## 2. Preop imaging: numbers to have ready

| Measure | Threshold | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| TT-TG | Over 20 mm abnormal, 15 to 20 borderline. MRI tends to read a few mm lower than CT | Amount of medialization |
| TT-PCL | Over 24 mm | Separates a truly lateral tubercle from a high TT-TG caused by trochlear dysplasia |
| Caton-Deschamps | Over 1.2 is alta | Whether to distalize. Alta also reduces trochlear engagement |
| Insall-Salvati | Over 1.2 is alta | Same |
| Patellar tilt (CT) | Over 20 degrees | Supports lateral lengthening |
| Trochlear dysplasia (Dejour) | A: crossing sign. B: plus supratrochlear spur. C: plus double contour. D: all plus a cliff | B and D may need a trochleoplasty, which is not planned |
| Chondral lesion location | | AMZ unloads the lateral and distal patella. It loads the medial and proximal patella and the medial trochlea. Lesions there argue against a steep AMZ |
| Coronal alignment, femoral anteversion, tibial torsion | | Large deformity points to other procedures |
| Tubercle apophysis | Must be closed | Open apophysis is a contraindication to TTO (recurvatum) |

## 5. Order of the case

1. Exam under anesthesia
2. Diagnostic arthroscopy
3. Graft prep on the back table (thaw, whipstitch both ends, size, pretension)
4. Lateral exposure and lengthening incisions. Leave open
5. TTO with AMZ, provisional fixation, check tracking, final fixation
6. MPFL: patellar anchors, femoral socket under fluoroscopy, pass the graft, tension and fix
7. Close the lateral retinaculum in its lengthened position
8. Final check through full ROM, closure

Why this order:
- MPFL tension depends on where the patella sits after the tubercle moves. If the MPFL is fixed first, medializing the tubercle afterward overtightens it.
- The lateral retinaculum is opened early so the patella can move medially, and closed last so its tension is set with the final position.

## 6. Steps in detail

### Arthroscopy
- Grade cartilage on the patella and trochlea and record location (this confirms the AMZ angle).
- View tracking from the superolateral portal through flexion.
- Look for loose bodies, a medial patellar facet impaction lesion, or an MPFL avulsion.
- Chondroplasty of unstable flaps.

### Lateral retinacular lengthening
1. Expose the lateral retinaculum from the distal edge of the vastus lateralis obliquus to the lateral border of the patellar tendon.
2. Incise the superficial oblique layer longitudinally close to the lateral patellar border.
3. Dissect sharply posteriorly between the superficial oblique and deep transverse layers for 1.5 to 2 cm. The layers are adherent, so use a fresh blade. Small rents in the deep layer are acceptable.
4. Incise the deep transverse layer at the posterior end of that dissection. Keep synovium intact if possible. This completes a coronal plane Z.
5. Do not extend the cut proximally into the vastus lateralis tendon. Detaching it causes quadriceps weakness and medial instability.
6. Cauterize the superior lateral geniculate artery near the proximal lateral patella. Hemarthrosis is the most common problem after lateral release.
7. Leave open until after the TTO and MPFL.
8. Closure at the end: knee at about 45 degrees with the patella seated in the trochlea. Sew the free edge of the deep layer (still attached to the patella) to the free edge of the superficial layer (still attached laterally). The layers usually slide 5 to 15 mm.
9. Check: tilt neutral in extension. If the patella can be everted to 90 degrees, it is over-lengthened.

### Tibial tubercle osteotomy with anteromedialization (T3 AMZ)

Plan the numbers before the incision:
- Medialization needed = preop TT-TG minus target (10 to 15 mm). Most cases need about 8 to 10 mm.
- Do not go below a TT-TG of about 10 mm. Over-medialization overloads the medial patellofemoral and medial tibiofemoral compartments.
- If the shingle slides a distance d along a cut angled at theta from the coronal plane: medial shift = d x cos(theta), anterior shift = d x sin(theta). At 45 degrees, 10 mm of slide gives about 7 mm each way. At 60 degrees, about 5 mm medial and 8.7 mm anterior. Confirm the angle convention on the T3 guide.
- Steeper cut (around 60 degrees) when the goal includes unloading a lateral or distal patellar lesion. Flatter (45 or less) when the goal is mainly medialization.

Steps:
1. Define the medial and lateral borders of the patellar tendon and its insertion. The tendon stays attached to the shingle.
2. Elevate tibialis anterior subperiosteally off the lateral tibia back to the posterolateral corner. Place the T3 posterior retractor there. This protects the anterior tibial artery and deep peroneal nerve where the cut exits.
3. Incise periosteum along the medial border of the planned shingle.
4. Place the tuberosity pin, set the guide to the planned angle, and pin the cutting block.
5. Make the oblique cut with the saw under irrigation, medial anterior to posterolateral. Shingle length about 6 to 8 cm. Taper it so the distal end exits the anterior cortex gradually with no step. A distal step-off is the main cause of fracture.
6. Keep the shingle about 1 cm thick proximally so it holds two screws.
7. Complete the proximal cuts with an osteotome, medial and lateral, just proximal to the tendon insertion, protecting the tendon. Do not enter the joint.
8. Mobilize the shingle and slide it anteromedially the planned distance. Measure with a ruler. Hold with two K-wires.
9. Check tracking through ROM. The tendon should not twist and the patella should not tilt medially.
10. Fix with two bicortical lag screws perpendicular to the cut, about 2 cm apart, heads countersunk.
11. Drill the posterior cortex in a controlled way and stop as soon as it is through. The popliteal artery, its trifurcation, and the tibial nerve lie directly behind the tibia at this level.
12. AP and lateral fluoroscopy: shingle position, screw length, no fracture.
13. Bone from the medial step can be packed into the lateral gap.
14. Do not close the anterior compartment fascia tightly.

### MPFL reconstruction

Anatomy to have in mind:
- The MPFL lies in layer 2 with the superficial MCL. The graft runs between layer 2 and the capsule (layer 3), outside the synovium.
- Femoral attachment is in the saddle between the adductor tubercle and the medial epicondyle. LaPrade 2007: about 10.6 mm proximal and 8.8 mm posterior to the medial epicondyle, and 1.9 mm anterior and 3.8 mm distal to the adductor tubercle.
- Schottle point on a perfect lateral: 1.3 mm anterior to the posterior femoral cortex line, 2.5 mm distal to the posterior origin of the medial condyle, proximal to the posterior end of Blumensaat's line.
- Patellar attachment: proximal half to two thirds of the medial patellar border, with fibers continuing onto the quadriceps tendon (MQTFL).
- The MPFL gives about 50 to 60% of the restraint to lateral translation from 0 to 30 degrees.

Steps:
1. Patellar incision about 3 cm along the proximal medial patella. Expose the medial border and lightly decorticate it.
2. Drill two sockets for the 3.9 mm SwiveLock anchors, 10 to 15 mm apart, in the proximal half of the medial border. Aim parallel to the articular surface, centered between the anterior cortex and the cartilage. Risks are patella fracture and articular breach.
3. Fix the graft to the patella with the two anchors.
4. Femoral incision 2 to 3 cm over the saddle. Palpate the adductor tubercle and medial epicondyle.
5. Under a perfect lateral fluoroscopy shot, place the 2.4 mm eyelet pin at the Schottle point. Aim proximal and anterior so it exits the anterolateral femur, away from the notch and trochlea.
6. Isometry check: loop a suture from the patellar fixation around the pin and take the knee through ROM. It should be tight from 0 to 30 degrees and slacken a little in deep flexion.
   - Tightens with flexion: pin too proximal. This is the most common error. Move it distal.
   - Tight in extension, very loose in flexion: pin too distal.
7. Ream to graft diameter (6 or 7 mm) to about 25 mm depth.
8. With a curved clamp, develop the plane between layer 2 and the capsule from the patellar incision to the femoral incision. Pass the graft through it.
9. Pull the graft into the femoral socket with the eyelet pin.
10. Tension with the knee at 30 to 45 degrees and the patella centered in the trochlea. Pull only enough to remove slack. Do not pull the patella medially.
11. Insert the 6 x 20 mm FastThread screw.
12. Check: full ROM, patella centered, lateral glide 1 to 2 quadrants with a firm endpoint, no medial tilt. Overtightening raises medial patellofemoral pressure and causes pain, stiffness, and medial subluxation.

### Closure
- Close the lateral retinaculum as above.
- Let the tourniquet down and get hemostasis before closing.
- Layered closure. Drain per attending.
- Hinged brace locked in extension, cryotherapy.

## 7. Structures at risk

| Step | Structure | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| TTO cut exiting posterolaterally | Anterior tibial artery, deep peroneal nerve | Subperiosteal elevation, T3 posterior retractor |
| TTO drilling the far cortex | Popliteal artery and trifurcation, tibial nerve | Controlled drilling, stop at the far cortex, measure |
| TTO | Anterior compartment | Leave fascia loose, check compartments postop |
| TTO | Proximal tibia fracture | Tapered distal cut, no step, protected weight bearing |
| Lateral lengthening | Superior lateral geniculate artery | Cauterize |
| Lateral lengthening | Vastus lateralis tendon | Stay distal to it |
| Medial incisions and tubercle | Infrapatellar branch of the saphenous nerve | Warn the patient about numbness lateral to the incision |
| Patellar sockets | Patella fracture, articular breach | Proximal half, parallel to the joint, centered |
| Femoral socket | Notch, trochlea, MCL | Aim proximal and anterior, check on fluoroscopy |
| Positioning | Common peroneal nerve on the other leg | Pad the fibular head |

## 8. Postop (default, confirm with Dr. Gladstone)

- Weight bearing is set by the TTO: touch down weight bearing with crutches in a hinged brace locked in extension for about 6 weeks. Advance once healing is seen on x-ray. Stetson 1997 reported proximal tibia fractures with immediate full weight bearing after a Fulkerson osteotomy.
- ROM: 0 to 90 degrees for the first 4 weeks, then progress.
- Quad sets, straight leg raises in the brace, patellar mobilization.
- X-rays at 2 and 6 weeks.
- DVT prophylaxis per attending.
- Return to sport around 6 to 9 months.
- Symptomatic screws are the most common reason for reoperation after TTO.

## 9. Consent discussion

Recurrent instability, stiffness, tibial fracture, delayed or nonunion of the tubercle, symptomatic hardware, compartment syndrome, DVT and PE, infection, wound breakdown over the tubercle, numbness from the saphenous branch, patella fracture, overcorrection with medial instability or medial patellofemoral arthritis, persistent anterior knee pain.

## 10. Facts to know for the case

- MPFL: 50 to 60% of restraint to lateral translation from 0 to 30 degrees.
- TT-TG over 20 mm was found in 56% of knees with patellar instability and 3% of controls (Dejour 1994).
- TT-PCL over 24 mm, Caton-Deschamps over 1.2, tilt over 20 degrees.
- Lateral lengthening vs release: Pagenstert 2012 found better outcomes and no medial instability with lengthening. Release causes iatrogenic medial instability.
- Fulkerson AMZ (1983): an oblique osteotomy that moves the tubercle anterior and medial, unloading the lateral and distal patella.
- AMZ is a poor choice for medial facet, proximal pole, or medial trochlear lesions.
- Most common MPFL technical error: femoral socket too proximal (graft tightens in flexion).
